## The Core Hypothesis

Every planet in our solar system is the same planet at a different age. Not metaphorically — structurally. The Sun does not merely warm planets. It transforms them, sequentially, over geological time, as its energy output increases across its main-sequence lifespan.

## The Habitable Zone as a Moving Window

As the Sun grows hotter, the habitable zone — the band where liquid water can persist on a planetary surface — migrates outward. What was once temperate becomes hostile. What is now frozen will one day be warm.

## Evidence and Speculation

This framework is part observation, part inference, part original hypothesis. The distinctions matter.

**OBSERVED:** Venus shows signs of ancient water. Mars has dried riverbeds billions of years old. The Sun's luminosity increases approximately 10% per billion years.

**INFERRED:** If the habitable zone moves outward, planets experience a window of habitability then lose it as the Sun brightens.

**SPECULATIVE:** The outer planets — currently frozen — represent future habitable candidates, not permanent wastelands.
