## Abstract

<Lead>
  Stand at the edge of the Valles Marineris on Mars - a canyon that would swallow the entire continental United States - and look up at a sky that is thin, cold, and almost airless. Whatever made this world, something finished with it. The volcanoes are silent. The magnetic field is gone. The oceans, if they existed, are locked in ice or lost to space. Mars is not a failed planet. It may be a finished one.

  This page presents a speculative cosmological hypothesis built on several real observations and published models. The central claim is original: the planets are not unrelated bodies orbiting a passive star. They may represent sequential stages of one outward-moving lifecycle - each one a world that was built, used, and vacated as the Sun's energy pushed the habitable window further from the centre. Earth is not the destination. It is the current address.
</Lead>

The strongest version of the article is not "this is proven," but "this is a unifying interpretation worth examining." The references below support recurring scientific inputs such as habitable-zone migration, Venus's potentially temperate past, the Grand Tack model, the Great Red Spot's unresolved behavior, and remnant-core exoplanets.

## The Iteration Model - Each Cycle Runs at a Different Scale

The solar system is not running one lifecycle. It is running nested, overlapping iterations simultaneously. Jupiter is this iteration's gas giant. Earth is the previous iteration's remnant. Venus the one before that. Mercury the oldest readable record.

Each iteration's gas giant was smaller than the current one - less disk material, younger Sun, smaller feeding zone. The rocky planets are fossils of previous cycles, their sizes reading backwards through time.

The mass budget problem that appears when comparing Jupiter (318×) to Mars (0.107×) dissolves entirely when one recognises Mars is not Jupiter's future remnant - it is the remnant of a much smaller, much older iteration's gas giant.

### The Mars Anomaly - Resolved by the Grand Tack

One genuine inconsistency in the iteration sequence: Earth's source body (~50–100 Earth masses) was larger than Mars's source body (~10–15 Earth masses), despite Mars being the more recent iteration. If iterations grow larger over time, this ordering is wrong.

The Grand Tack hypothesis (Walsh et al., 2011) offers a mainstream model that helps with this mismatch. Within the first 10 million years of the solar system, Jupiter migrated inward from roughly 3.5 AU to around 1.5 AU before Saturn's gravitational influence reversed its course. In that model, material is swept away from the Mars orbital zone before Mars's source body can fully accrete. That means Mars can remain the outlier without forcing every iteration to scale monotonically. In the logic of this article, Mars becomes the interrupted transition rather than the disproving one.

## Open Problems - Where the Theory Needs Development

An honest assessment of the unresolved physical problems. These do not invalidate the framework but represent the work that remains.

1. **The moon collision explosion mechanism is unmodelled.** Collision energy from even a large moon may be insufficient to overcome Jupiter's gravitational binding energy (~10³¹ J vs ~10³⁶ J required). A cascade effect or compound mechanism is needed.

2. **Jupiter's core is diffuse.** Juno mission data shows rocky and metallic material dissolved throughout 30–40% of the planet's volume rather than a compact separable core. What remains after ejection may not be as clean as the theory requires.

3. **The gas giant to rocky planet transition timescale is unspecified.** The explosive mechanism needs a physical pathway that can operate on a geologically rapid timescale.

4. **Saturn's 285 moon count includes many tiny recently-discovered irregular moons.** Whether these participate in the lifecycle mechanism the same way large structural moons do requires clarification.

## Speculative Extension - Venus as the Previous Earth

This section is clearly labelled as speculative philosophy built on the cosmological framework. It is not evidenced science. It is an exploration of what the framework could imply - and the implication is not a comfortable one.

Venus may have sustained liquid water or temperate conditions for far longer than once assumed in some models. In this article's framework, that makes Venus the remnant of the most recently completed gas giant transition before Earth's source body - which means Venus is not simply a cautionary climate story. It is a portrait of what Earth becomes. Not eventually. On schedule.

<SpeculativeCallout label="Speculative - not evidenced">
  The Cambrian Explosion (~540 million years ago) - Earth's most dramatic and unexplained biological acceleration - coincides precisely with the closure of Venus's last habitable atmospheric zone. The theory does not replace evolution. Unicellular life originated independently on Earth over 3 billion years. The speculation concerns only the sudden acceleration of complex multicellular body plans in the Cambrian - and whether the arrival of accumulated biological complexity from elsewhere could account for a pace that natural selection alone has not fully explained. This remains speculation built on a framework that is itself unconfirmed.

What is not speculative is the sequence itself. Venus lost its surface habitability. Its sky, for a window of perhaps 100 to 200 million years afterward, remained Earth-like at altitude - pressures and temperatures in the cloud layer that a human could survive without a pressure suit, suspended above a surface already become uninhabitable. Then that window closed too. The question the framework raises is not whether Venus was once like Earth. It is whether Earth is currently like Venus was - and whether the beings that may have lived through Venus's closing window asked the same questions about their origin that we are now asking about ours.

The connection between Venus's three atmospheric zones and the near-universal ancient human tripartite cosmology - Heaven above, Earth in the middle, Underworld below - is noted as a structural convergence whose origin is unknown. It may be coincidence. It may be the residue of a cultural memory so old it predates writing, carried forward through oral tradition from people who watched their sky change and built their cosmology around the last three places something could survive. Its interpretation is, and should remain, left to the reader.

## Implication - Life as a Passenger, Not an Accident

If the planetary lifecycle is real, and if intelligence tends to emerge given sufficient time in a habitable window, then the solar system has been building toward greater biological complexity for billions of years - not through any guiding intention, but through the simple mechanics of each transition leaving a larger, more capable platform than the last.

Each iteration gives the universe slightly more time to produce intelligence that escapes the window entirely. Jupiter's future remnant will be larger than Earth, further from the Sun, with a freshly reheated core and icy moon water delivery. The most habitable platform this solar system will have ever produced - if life is already in the loop.

The Fermi Paradox's silence may not be the silence of absence. It may be the silence of transit - intelligence that appears on one platform and moves to the next before the signal from the first one has even arrived.

## Why It Matters If It Is True

A theory does not need to be confirmed to change the question. This one changes it completely.

If the Solar Lifecycle Theory is wrong in its mechanism but right in its intuition - that the planets are not a random collection of objects but a sequence with a direction - then the implications extend far beyond cosmology. They extend to what we think we are doing here.

Every civilisation that has ever looked up has assumed, in some form, that it was the first. The intended occupant of a stage built specifically for it. The lifecycle framework says something different: that intelligence is the solar system's way of not losing the thread between iterations. That what we are accumulating - knowledge, biology, the ability to recognise what is happening - is not for us. It is for whoever builds the bridge to the next platform.

Jupiter's future remnant will be a rocky world larger than Earth, sitting in a habitable zone warmed by a sub-giant Sun, with a freshly reheated core and an outer system full of water. If there is no one on it when that window opens, the sequence continues without a witness. If there is, whatever they know will have come, in part, from here.

The theory is unconfirmed. The mechanism is unmodelled. The physical problems listed above are real, and they may be fatal to the framework in its current form. But the question the theory poses survives even if the theory does not: *what are the planets for?* Science has a good answer about how they formed. It does not yet have a satisfying answer about whether their arrangement, their sequence, their timing - means anything at all.

This article is a first attempt to suggest that it might.
